# Phase 12 — HUD Rework

Presentation only: the rules engine, the drivers and the Edge Functions are untouched. The in-game screen is now one full-bleed diorama with a single HUD layer over it, laid out like a modern digital tabletop: player banners across the top, an icon-only resource tray at the bottom, a left rail of square icon buttons, round action buttons bottom right, and every reference or status panel as a dark translucent overlay on the wooden table. The old parchment sidebar, bottom bar and text button row are gone; their last state is the git tag `hud-legacy`.

## 1. Layer and grid (`src/hud/HudLayer.tsx`)

`GameScreen` renders `Board3D` in an absolutely positioned `<main>` that fills the viewport and `HudLayer` over it. The layer is a CSS grid (`.hud-layer` in `src/styles/hud.css`): an 84 px top row, an 88 px bottom row, an 80 px left column, 16 px outer margin. Two `.hud-scrim` gradients (rendered first in the layer, so they paint under everything else) fade the table into the top and bottom bands: without them the banners and the tray read as hard-edged slabs pasted over the diorama. `pointer-events: none` on the layer; only interactive children (`.hud-interactive`, the buttons, the panels) take pointer events, so the canvas keeps its raycasting everywhere else.

Regions: `hud-banners` (top, `PlayerBanner` × N, flex-wrap), `hud-rail` + `hud-side` (left), `hud-bottom` (a three-column row: `BuildCostCard` · `ResourceTray` with the dice widget, the mode hint, the trade response and the module strip above it · `ActionButtons`). Overlays: the status line (`data-testid="banner"`, docked under the top row), `TurnBanner`, `EventToast`, `HelpTip`, and the Wayfarers pill (raider counter, event deck) at the top right.

Minimum viewport is 1280 × 720. Banners shrink from 300 px to 232 px so four fit beside the camera button; below the minimum they collapse to portrait + VP (`@media` in `hud.css`). No HUD element covers the central hex at 1280 × 720.

## 2. Tokens (`src/styles/hud.css`)

`--hud-panel` (a warm near-black, not a neutral grey: the chrome sits on a walnut table and a cool panel reads as a foreign UI layer), `--hud-panel-solid`, `--hud-border`, `--hud-radius`, `--hud-text`, `--hud-text-dim`, `--hud-accent` (gold), `--hud-font` (Oswald / Barlow Condensed / system fallback; no font is downloaded), `--hud-parchment` (the cost card only) and the `--player-*` tints. Rules: every panel is `.hud-panel` (dark, translucent, blurred); numerals are `.hud-num` (white, bold, condensed); player colours appear only as banner borders, ribbons and piece tints; parchment is reserved for the cost card. `.hud-dark` restyles the shared `Button`, `Modal`, checkboxes and the ink text tokens for dark chrome, so the existing dialogs, sheets and prompts (discard, gold, resource picker, the knight menu, the improvement and progress sheets, the Crown prompts, the fish sheet, the end screen, the handoff) simply swapped `parchment` for `hud-panel hud-dark`.

Banner tints (`HUD_PLAYER_COLOR`) match the pieces on the table; argent (white) gets a slate ribbon so the white name stays legible.

## 3. Components

* `PlayerBanner` — 300 × 84 (shrinks to 232 wide), laid out like a table-top scoreboard. VP badge (36 px gold circle, top-left of the portrait), gold turn marker (200 ms scale-in) for the acting player, 56 × 56 portrait with a 3 px player-colour border, two pills hanging under it (Crown: active knights / defence; base: knights played / longest road), the name ribbon (`clip-path` flag, bot glyph with the difficulty tag underneath, presence dot online, and under Crown & Castle the three city improvement tracks as 7 × 19 px pip columns at the ribbon's end — one pip per level in the track's colour, the third pip ringed gold, the whole column framed gold while the player holds that metropolis), and the stat block of numeral over a 15 px glyph: Roads · Largest Army (base) or Defence (Crown) · Knights · Dev / Progress cards · Cards in hand. A column glows gold while the player holds the matching title; a zero column keeps its glyph as a label and its numeral recedes (`data-zero`). Hovering a banner outlines that player's pieces on the board (`onHover` → `Board3D`'s `highlight`: a ring under each settlement and city, a bar along each road and ship, in the player's colour). A thin gold progress bar runs under the ribbon for the length of a bot's thinking pause. Wayfarers badges, island pennants and the host's "let a bot play" control ride in an extra row under the block (those variants carry state the five columns do not cover). Hover for 300 ms → the detail tip (goods, progress cards, tracks and abilities, walls, pieces left, fish/coins/guards/spice/deliveries). Banners are ordered by seat with the local player first. Test ids: `player-row-<id>` (`aria-current` while acting), `vp-<id>`, `knights-<id>`, `defense-<id>`, `tracks-<id>`, `bot-badge-<id>`, `presence-<id>`, `thinking-<id>`, `emote-bubble-<id>`.
* `ResourceTray` — 560 × 58, eight cells (Wood, Clay, Wool, Grain, Ore, Cloth, Coin, Paper; the last three only under Crown & Castle): 21 px numeral + 24 px icon, no label; zero cells drop to 0.32 alpha (0.28 on the icon) so a hand of zeros recedes and any non-zero count reads on its own. `data-testid="hand"` wraps the five resource cells (`[data-resource]`, `aria-label="N Wood"`), `commodities` the three commodity cells, so the existing specs read them unchanged. While the trade panel is composing an offer a resource cell is clickable and adds one card to the "give" side (`TradeDialog` accepts a controlled `give`). The cells register the `hand:<card>` anchors for flying cards. Before the hotseat handoff the tray shows dim icons with no numbers and no `hand` wrapper.
* `LeftRail` + `SidePanel` — seven 48 px buttons (Chat, Emote, Log, Stats, Rules, Settings, Leave; `rail-<key>`, the Settings one keeps `data-testid="settings"`). Each opens a 280 px dark panel that slides in from the left (200 ms), never a modal; Esc or ✕ closes. Chat rides the driver when it offers `sendChat` / `subscribeChat` (online: the `send-chat` Edge Function writes `game_chat`, published on the game's Realtime channel and readable by members only; the layer maps each line's `playerId` to the banner's name and colour) and stays on this screen in hotseat. Emote shows a bubble over your banner for 2.5 s. Log is the old log panel, newest first; while closed the same list is rendered `sr-only` so `data-testid="log"` is always in the page. Stats: a per-player table, the titles and a 2–12 roll histogram. Rules: this scenario's summary and special rules, build costs, titles, the Crown tracks, the active Wayfarers variants. Settings: the old menu's body (`SettingsBody` in `src/hud/SettingsMenu.tsx`, same test ids) plus the key list. Leave: the online escape hatches (`hand-to-bot`, `reclaim-seat`, `abandon`) and a confirmed "Leave the table".
* `DiceWidget` — above the tray's left end: the current roll (the DOM dice tray with `dice-tray`, `red-die`, `event-die`, `event-card`, moved from `anim/effects.tsx`; 40 px faces with the total beside them, `dice-total`) and a history toggle (`dice-history`) that lists the last six rolls with the event die.
* `BuildCostCard` — 250 px parchment card, tilted −2°, bottom left beside the rail, **closed by default** behind a Build toggle (`build-costs`, `aria-expanded`, a gold badge with the number of affordable rows while closed; `B` toggles it; the state is remembered in `localStorage` under `katan.hud.costCard`). The card is a reference: every affordable build is already marked on the board (§8). Rows: Road, Ship (Tides), Settlement, City, Development card (base), City wall, Knight, Promote knight, City improvement (Crown). Each row: name + note on the left, the cost as resource icons on the right; an affordable row gets the gold left bar and is a button (`build-road`, `build-ship`, `build-settlement`, `build-city`, `buy-dev`, `wall`, `knight`, `knight-promote`, `improve`) that enters the placement mode, buys the card, or opens the improvement sheet; a disabled row carries its reason in `title`. Rows come from the pure `costRows` in `src/hud/model.ts`.
* `ActionButtons` — bottom right: Undo (46 px, `undo-build`, only while `UNDO_BUILD` is legal, docs/rules.md §5.6; `U` too), Cards (46 px, `cards`, with a count badge; opens `CardsPanel` above the tray: dev cards as `dev-<type>` buttons or progress cards as `progress-<card>` buttons opening the progress sheet), Trade (68 px, `trade`) and the primary slot: Roll (`roll`, pulsing) during your roll phase, "Done" (`special-build-done`) in the special build phase, else End turn (`end-turn`, dimmed until legal, pulsing gold when it is the only action left — `onlyEndTurnLeft`). While the animation queue drains the slot is Skip (`skip`).
* `ModuleStrip` — the dark strip above the tray's right half for what the base layout has no slot for: Tides' Move ship, the Wayfarers buttons (`src/hud/WayfarersActions.tsx`), the Diplomat / Smith one-answer buttons, Withdraw offer.
* Trading (docs/rules.md §9.1): a responder's `TradeResponse` above the tray has Accept, Counter (an inline give / want form, `counter-offer`, `send-counter`; a sent counter shows under the offer, `my-counter`) and Decline; the offerer sees `CounterOffers` in the same slot, one line per counter with an Accept button (`accept-counter-<id>`), and the status line lists who countered (`countered`). Renders nothing otherwise. Knights are reached by clicking a knight on the board (the interaction layer already exposes your knights whenever no mode is active), so the old "Knights" button is gone; Promote knight on the cost card enters the same pick.
* `TurnBanner` — dark panel, gold display text, the player's colour as a left bar; appears at 22 % from the top, shrinks to 60 % after 1.5 s and docks under the top row before fading (2.6 s total; skipped when animations are off).
* `EventToast` — 360 px max dark panel with a white tab, docked in a column under the camera button at the top right (it used to sit centred at 20 % from the top, on the status line and over the far side of the board), four seconds each, queued: roll results (with the event die), event cards, robber and pirate moves, island bonuses, raids, the boot, barbarian attacks, Defender awards, metropolises, opponents' progress cards, the graphics watchdog notice. The connection notice uses the same chrome at 12 %.
* `HelpTip` — one provider at the end of the layer; `useTipHandlers(content, delay)` gives any element mouse/focus handlers. Content comes from `src/hud/hudCopy.ts` (rail, actions, costs, tracks, titles, stats, table props).
* `icons/` — an original monochrome 24 × 24 set (resources, commodities, pieces, stats, rail glyphs, actions) filled with `currentColor`; resource glyphs take a material tint in the tray and the cost card.

## 4. On-table props (`src/board/props/`)

Rendered inside the diorama through `Board3D`'s `children`, positioned by the pure `layout.ts` from the board's bounds (unit tested):

* `BankDecks` — one flat stack per resource (and commodity) down the right edge on a dark leather mat with a "Bank" label at its head (`bank-label`), height by count, hover for the count (drei `Html`); the development deck or the three progress decks after them with a permanent count label (`deck-count`). The board's projector resolves the `bank` and `deck` anchor keys to these spots so card flights still land on the bank.
* `PiecePiles` — each player's unplaced roads, settlements, cities, ships (Tides) and level-1 knights (Crown) as the board's own GLB figurines at 0.7 of their size, clustered by their seat edge (roads side by side like stacked planks, then a row per kind); they shrink as pieces are placed. Seats are counted from the viewer (`seatFrom`), so every player sees their own pieces on the near edge in front of them, the others round the table. One model load per kind and colour, cloned per item; a tinted block stands in until it loads.
* `BarbarianTrack` — Crown & Castle: a chain of four sea hexes hooked onto the board's top-left corner (`barbarianTrackLayout(hexes)`: from the neighbour left of the board's top-left-most hex, stepping left and up-left alternately, so no board can overlap it), the way the fleet's tiles attach to a real board; seven markers spaced along the chain from the open sea at the far end to a stone landing on the hex against the board, the longship easing 600 ms one marker per fleet step, and a screen-space pill group hanging below the far hex (`Barbarian fleet N/7`, `Active knights N`, `Barbarian strength N`) plus the last event die. The pill group carries `data-testid="fleet-track"` with `data-position` / `data-attacks`, and `fleet-odds`, `fleet-attacks`, `fleet-ship` (sr-only) for the specs. It was a straight lane along the bottom edge beside the dice tray; the camera now looks squarely at the board, so the top-left corner is in view.
* `ImprovementCard` — Crown & Castle: each player's city improvement chart as one parchment card lying in front of their piece pile (`cardLayout`, 0.82 × 1.04, always facing the camera): a band in the player's colour with their name, then the three tracks as columns of five cells filled to the level reached in the track's colour, the third cell ringed gold, a gold crown over a track whose metropolis the player holds. Hover for the levels; the viewer's own card is clickable (and carries a focusable `improvement-card` button) and opens the improvement sheet, so improving a track is one click on the table rather than a trip through the build cost card.
* Dice stay in the scene; the event die already lands beside them under Crown.

The "Reset view" button became a camera icon at the top-right corner (still `reset-view`, still owned by `Board3D`).

## 5. Behaviour and pacing

* Every HUD numeral is a `Numeral` (`src/hud/primitives.tsx`): on change it bumps (scale 1.3 → 1, 250 ms) and floats a `+N` / `−N`; nothing jumps.
* `hud:tick` fires on every bump and `hud:open` when a panel or the cards panel opens; `installHudSounds` in `src/game/sound.ts` plays a soft tick / breath for them while sound is on (ticks are rate-limited to one per 60 ms).
* Keys: `E` end turn, `T` trade, `L` toggle the log, `B` toggle the build-cost card, `Space` skip, `Esc` closes the side panel, the cards panel, the trade panel or the targeting mode (in that order). Keys are ignored while typing.
* Bot turns: the gold progress bar under the bot's ribbon runs for the thinking pause.

## 6. Tests

* `test/hud.test.ts` — banner statistics per module, cost-card rows and reasons, the end-turn pulse rule, the status line, the dice history and histogram, the prop layout.
* e2e: `crown.spec.ts` now reads the cost-card rows (`knight`, `knight-promote`, `improve`, `wall`), the banner's `knights-` / `defense-` cells, the `cards` button's reason and the fleet pills; `fullgame.spec.ts` opens the Cards panel when the button's count is non-zero. The other specs pass unchanged because the test ids were preserved. Visual baselines were regenerated: the canvas is now full-bleed.

## 7. Decisions and deviations

* The cost card sits right of the rail (x = 64 px) rather than at 16 px: at 720 p the rail's lower buttons and a nine-row card would overlap. It is closed by default (§8): open, it can cover a legal target near the table's bottom-left corner; the camera can be dragged, and the specs click central vertices. The specs that drive its rows (`fullgame`, `online`, `crown`) open it through `localStorage` before the game loads.
* Banners shrink (400 → 288 px) before they wrap so four fit at 1280 wide beside the camera button; the spec's flex-wrap remains for five and six players.
* Roll is a round button in the End turn slot during the roll phase (the spec lists only Trade and End turn); the specs and the greedy full-game policy click `roll`.
* `.hud-bottom` pins its row to `--hud-bottom` instead of letting it size to content. An auto row grows to the tallest child, and because the band is aligned to the bottom of the viewport that growth pushes every child in the row *off* the bottom edge rather than upward — the action labels, the tray and the cost card all went past the viewport when the round buttons grew. The band is sized to hold the buttons plus the labels that hang under them.
* The barbarian lane's pills (§4) are anchored to a world point above the lane's sea end. On a large Phase 8 board, or after the camera is dragged, that point can project outside the canvas and the pills were clipped by the window edge — "Active knights" rendered as "ctive knights". `BarbarianTrack` passes drei's `calculatePosition` to keep the projection but hold the result inside the viewport, clear of the bands, the left rail and the dice widget.
* The legal-target rings pulse from a `useFrame` in `InteractionLayer`. That pulse is now gated on the quality preset's idle motion, as the tiles and props already were: with animations off nothing on the board should move, and the ungated version left 40-odd rings mid-animation in every frame. That put a ~3.8% frame-to-frame noise floor under `e2e/visual.spec.ts`, which forced its tolerance wide enough that a whole palette overhaul passed unnoticed. With the pulse gated, two consecutive captures of the fixture are pixel-identical, so the spec tightened from `maxDiffPixelRatio` 0.03 at Playwright's default per-pixel threshold to 0.01 at threshold 0.05 — measured as the loosest per-pixel threshold that still catches a single terrain reverting to its pre-ladder colour. The idle rings also dropped from 0.7/0.6/0.5 opacity to 0.42/0.38/0.34; forty of them at full strength read as stickers on the board.
* Wayfarers and Tides have no slot in the spec's layout; their per-player badges ride under the banner's stat block and their actions in the module strip above the tray. The raider counter and event deck count sit in a pill under the top row.
* Chat's transport is the `game_chat` table (`supabase/migrations/20260920120000_chat.sql`): no client writes, one `send-chat` function, a members-only read policy, the table in the Realtime publication. The driver merges the initial fetch with inserts by id, so a line that arrives before the fetch is not doubled.
* The tilt-shift post effect is gone (docs/props.md §6): on the full-bleed canvas its blur band fell across the banners' side of the board and the near piles.
* The old components (`BottomBar`, `PlayersPanel`, `LogPanel`, `CrownBadges`, `CrownActions`, `wayfarers/Badges`, `SettingsMenu` popover) were deleted; `git tag hud-legacy` marks the last commit that had them.

## 9. Camera and hover

The default view is the same for every seat: squarely in front of the board (azimuth 0, so the hex rows run across the screen) at 56° elevation, the way a table reads when everyone has the board turned toward them. Number tokens and the port discs are turned to face it (`LABEL_TURN` in `Camera.tsx`: a label baked onto a cylinder cap has its top along local +x, so π/2 + azimuth points it at the camera), so the numbers always read upright. It frames the land plus the first ring of sea and the props just off it (`cameraBounds` in `layout3d.ts`: the land bounds with radius `landBounds.radius + 2.4`, never more than the whole board's), then the table round that: `CAMERA_TABLE_MARGIN` (1.5) more radius for the piles, the bank and the fleet's track, with the framed centre pushed `CAMERA_TABLE_SHIFT` (0.8) toward the camera so the viewer's own pile and improvement card on the near edge are in the frame rather than under the bottom band. The fog follows the camera (`DepthFog` in `Board3D.tsx`): its near plane sits `FOG_NEAR` (1.6) board radii past the camera's distance to the board, so the board is never fogged at any zoom and only the table beyond it fades; the fixed 4–6 radii it used to be swallowed the whole board when zoomed out. Hovering a settlement target on a port still shows the port's ratio and resource in a chip above it (`port-tip`).

## 8. Board-first play

With no targeting mode chosen during the action or special build phase, `computeTargets` offers every legal road, ship, settlement and city as a target (`Interaction.tsx`), so a player builds by clicking the board without opening the cost card. The engine only lists a build the player can pay for, so the rest-state targets are exactly the affordable ones. A coastal edge that could take a road or a ship shows the road; the Ship row (ship mode) narrows the edges to ships. Rest-state rings and bars are drawn at 0.6 of a chosen mode's opacity (`subtle`), and a hovered target still shows its ghost piece. The cost-card rows keep their modes for narrowing and for the specs, which click a row and then a target.

`buildReason` also answers for setup and Road Building now: those placements are legal builds but go on the board, so the rows read "Place your starting pieces on the board" / "Place the free roads on the board" instead of lighting up. Before this the Settlement row was affordable during setup and clicking it did nothing useful.
